Care guide
Not eating?
Check first
Is she in pre-shed? Skin goes dull or dusty, eyes may cloud slightly. It is normal to refuse food 3–10 days during a shed — do not force feed.
Check second
Temperatures correct? Warm hide floor 88–92°F (31–33°C), cool side 72–77°F (22–25°C). Too cold and she cannot digest — appetite disappears first.
Check third
Any recent changes? New enclosure, rearranged décor, a new pet nearby. Leopard geckos need 1–2 weeks to settle after any disruption.
When to act
Over 14 days without eating, not in shed, no recent stressors, and weight is dropping — book a vet visit.
Weight dropping?
Under 8% loss
Watch closely. Weigh every 3 days. Check temperatures and supplement schedule. Try a different feeder insect — dubia roaches often restart appetite.
8–15% loss
Get a vet appointment. Possible parasites, bacterial infection, or husbandry issue. Bring your full weight log.
Over 15% loss
Vet urgently. Possible cryptosporidiosis, severe parasites, or systemic illness. Do not wait.
Stuck shed?
Toes or tail tip
15–20 minute warm (not hot) water soak. Gently roll shed off with a damp cotton bud — never pull. A dark or purple toe means circulation is cut off: vet today.
Eye caps
Ensure a humid hide is available (damp moss in a small box). Soak again. If retained after the next shed, a vet visit is needed.
Prevention
Always keep a humid hide — damp paper towel or moss in a small tupperware with an entry hole. Especially important the week before an expected shed.
Supplements
Plain calcium (no D3)
Under 12 months: every feed. Adult: every other feed. Dust lightly on feeder insects before offering.
Calcium with D3
Under 12 months: twice a week. Adult: once a week. Do not exceed this — D3 is fat-soluble and toxic in high doses.
Multivitamin
Once per week at any age, alternating with the calcium + D3 day.
Go to the vet now
Mucus from nose or mouth — respiratory infection or mouth rot. Within 48 hours.
Head tilting, wobbling, circling, or star-gazing — neurological. Enigma morphs have higher risk; any morph warrants a vet visit.
Bloody or very liquid stool with no urate — possible cryptosporidiosis or severe infection.
Swollen limbs, jaw, or distended belly — possible metabolic bone disease, abscess, or egg-binding in females.
Over 15% weight loss — always warrants a same-week vet visit, no exceptions.
